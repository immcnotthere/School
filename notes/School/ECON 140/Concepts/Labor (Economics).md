# Labor (L)

---

## Definition

> **Labor** is the human input to production — workers' time, effort, and skills. In trade theory it is denoted **L**.

Labor is the foundational factor of production across trade models. It is the **only** factor in the [[Ricardian Model]], and the **[[Mobile Factor]]** in the [[Specific Factors Model]] — meaning it can flow freely between sectors in response to wage differentials.

---

## Labor Across Trade Models

| Model | Role of Labor | Mobility |
|-------|--------------|----------|
| [[Ricardian Model]] | Only factor of production | N/A (one sector per country in autarky) |
| [[Specific Factors Model]] | Mobile factor — moves between sectors | ✅ Fully mobile (short run) |
| [[Hecksher-Ohlin Model|Heckscher-Ohlin Model]] | Mobile factor alongside [[Capital]] | ✅ Fully mobile (long run) |

## Labor in the [[Specific Factors Model]]

### Role

Labor is the **mobile factor** — it allocates itself between both sectors to equalize wages:

$$Q_C = Q_C(K,\ L_C) \qquad Q_F = Q_F(T,\ L_F)$$

### Labor Market Clearing

Total labor supply $\bar{L}$ is fixed:

$$L_C + L_F = \bar{L}$$

Labor moves until wages are equalized across sectors:

$$w = P_C \cdot MPL_C = P_F \cdot MPL_F$$

This is the **wage equalization condition** — the core of labor market equilibrium in the SF model.

---

## Marginal Product of Labor

Under [[Diminishing Marginal Returns]]:

$$MPL_C = \frac{\partial Q_C}{\partial L_C}, \qquad MPL_F = \frac{\partial Q_F}{\partial L_F}$$

- $MPL_C \downarrow$ as $L_C \uparrow$ (holding K fixed)
- $MPL_F \downarrow$ as $L_F \uparrow$ (holding T fixed)
- [[Factor Complementarity]]: $MPL_C \uparrow$ when K increases; $MPL_F \uparrow$ when T increases

### Connection to the PPF

The slope of the [[Production Possibility Frontier]] is the ratio of marginal products:

$$\left|\frac{dQ_F}{dQ_C}\right| = \frac{MPL_F}{MPL_C}$$

---

## Wages and the Effect of Trade

### Nominal Wage

From the labor market equilibrium condition:

$$w = P_C \cdot MPL_C = P_F \cdot MPL_F$$

When the SOE opens to trade and Clothing expands ($P_C \uparrow$, $L_C \uparrow$):

- $MPL_C \downarrow$ (more workers in Clothing, diminishing returns)
- $MPL_F \uparrow$ (fewer workers in Food, rising marginal product)
- The nominal wage **rises**, but by less than $P_C$

### Real Wage — The Ambiguity

From [[Econ 140 – Lecture 9: The Specific Factors Model|Lecture 9]], the effect on labor is **ambiguous**:

$$\frac{w}{P_C} \downarrow \qquad \text{(real wage in terms of Clothing falls)}$$

$$\frac{w}{P_F} \uparrow \qquad \text{(real wage in terms of Food rises)}$$

The net effect depends on **workers' consumption basket** — the share of income spent on Clothing vs. Food:

$$\Delta \text{real wage} \gtrless 0 \iff \text{depends on preferences}$$

> **Conclusion:** Labor is the ambiguous factor — unlike the specific factors, it neither clearly wins nor clearly loses from trade.

## Worker Mobility in Practice

From [[Econ 140 – Lecture 9: The Specific Factors Model|Lecture 9]], the SF model's assumption of perfect labor mobility is an approximation:

| Dimension | Reality |
|-----------|---------|
| **Speed** | Most U.S. workers find new employment within **4 years** after displacement |
| **Cost** | Switching occupations carries a permanent **18% average wage drop** |
| **Age** | Younger workers are more mobile; older workers face higher switching costs |
| **Skill type** | General skills → more mobile; job-specific skills → less mobile |

This suggests that in the **short run**, Labor behaves more like a [[Specific Factor]]; in the **long run**, it approximates a [[Mobile Factor]].

## Labor in the [[Ricardian Model]]

In the [[Ricardian Model]], Labor is the **only** factor. All comparative advantage arises from differences in **labor productivity** across countries:

$$a_{LC}, a_{LF} \quad \text{(unit labor requirements)}$$

$$\text{Comparative advantage in C if: } \frac{a_{LC}}{a_{LF}} < \left(\frac{a_{LC}}{a_{LF}}\right)^*$$

Because labor is the only factor and has **constant marginal product** (CRS with one factor), the [[Production Possibility Frontier]] is **linear** and **complete specialization** is possible.

In the [[Specific Factors Model]], the declining $MPL$ in each sector (due to fixed K and T) makes the PPF **concave** and leads to **incomplete specialization**.

---

## Key Equations

$$L_C + L_F = \bar{L} \quad \text{(labor market clearing)}$$

$$w = P_C \cdot MPL_C = P_F \cdot MPL_F \quad \text{(wage equalization)}$$

$$MPL_C = \frac{\partial Q_C}{\partial L_C}, \quad MPL_F = \frac{\partial Q_F}{\partial L_F}$$

$$\left|\frac{dQ_F}{dQ_C}\right| = \frac{MPL_F}{MPL_C} \quad \text{(PPF slope)}$$

$$\frac{w}{P_C} \downarrow, \quad \frac{w}{P_F} \uparrow \quad \text{(ambiguous real wage effect)}$$
