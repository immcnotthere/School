# Small Open Economy (SOE)

---

## Definition

> A **small open economy** is a country that takes [[world prices]] as given. Nothing a small open economy does unilaterally can affect its world relative prices.

In other words, the SOE is a **price taker** on international markets — it faces a perfectly elastic demand for its exports and perfectly elastic supply of its imports at the prevailing world price.

---

## Key Properties

| Property | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Price taker** | Cannot influence [[world prices]] regardless of its domestic production or consumption decisions |
| **Open** | Participates in international trade (imports and exports) |
| **Small** | Share of world trade is negligible — no market power |

---

## Contrast: Small vs. Large Open Economy

| Feature | Small Open Economy | [[Large Open Economy]] |
|---------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Price setting | Takes world prices as given | Can influence world prices |
| [[Terms of Trade]] | Exogenous | Endogenous |
| Optimal tariff | Zero (no market power) | Positive (can improve ToT) |
| Trade policy effect | Purely domestic redistribution | Can shift world prices |

## SOE in the [[Specific Factors Model]]

### Setup

The SOE faces an exogenous world relative price $\left(\frac{P_C}{P_F}\right)^T$.

Suppose the autarky relative price satisfies:

$$\left(\frac{P_C}{P_F}\right)^A < \left(\frac{P_C}{P_F}\right)^T$$

Clothing is relatively **cheaper domestically** than on world markets.

### What Happens When the SOE Opens to Trade?

1. The SOE has a [[Comparative Advantage]] in **Clothing**
2. It **exports Clothing** and **imports Food**
3. Labor shifts: $L_C \uparrow$, $L_F \downarrow$
4. [[Incomplete Specialization]] — the [[Specific Factors Model]] prevents complete specialization because:

$$MPL_F \uparrow \text{ as } L \text{ leaves Food} \Rightarrow \text{Incentive to switch attenuates}$$

### Production and Consumption Points

- **Production point** moves along the [[Production Possibility Frontier]] toward more Clothing
- **Consumption point** moves to a higher [[indifference curve]] via trade
- The gap between production and consumption = **volume of trade**

$$\text{Exports of C} = Q_C^{\text{produced}} - C_C^{\text{consumed}}$$
$$\text{Imports of F} = C_F^{\text{consumed}} - Q_F^{\text{produced}}$$

---

## SOE Equilibrium Condition

In a competitive SOE, [[labor market]] equilibrium requires wages to be equalized across sectors:

$$w = P_C \cdot MPL_C = P_F \cdot MPL_F$$

This ensures no worker has an incentive to switch sectors.

Rearranging:

$$\frac{MPL_F}{MPL_C} = \frac{P_C}{P_F}$$

The slope of the [[Production Possibility Frontier]] equals the **relative price of Clothing**.

## Distributional Effects of Opening Trade (SOE)

From [[Econ 140 – Lecture 9: The Specific Factors Model|Lecture 9]]:

| Factor | Sector | Effect of Opening Trade |
|--------|--------|------------------------|
| [[Capital]] (K) | Clothing (export) | ✅ Real return **rises** |
| [[Land]] (T) | Food (import-competing) | ❌ Real return **falls** |
| [[Labor]] (L) | Mobile | ❓ **Ambiguous** — depends on consumption basket |

## Why "Small" Matters for Policy

Because the SOE cannot affect world prices:

- A **tariff** raises the domestic price of imports but does **not** improve the [[Terms of Trade]]
- The tariff's only effect is domestic redistribution (protecting specific factors in the import sector at the expense of consumers and export-sector factors)
- There is **no optimal tariff argument** for a SOE — free trade is the welfare-maximizing policy in the absence of other distortions

$$\text{SOE welfare} \Rightarrow \text{maximized at free trade}$$

---

## Real-World Examples

Countries commonly modeled as SOEs:

- Small export-oriented economies (e.g. Singapore, New Zealand, Denmark)
- Developing countries in primary commodity markets (they face world commodity prices they cannot control)

Large economies like the **US, EU, China** are typically modeled as [[Large Open Economy|large open economies]] because their trade volumes are large enough to affect world prices.

## Key Equations

$$\left(\frac{P_C}{P_F}\right)^A < \left(\frac{P_C}{P_F}\right)^T \Rightarrow \text{Export C, Import F}$$

$$w = P_C \cdot MPL_C = P_F \cdot MPL_F \quad \text{(labor market clearing)}$$

$$\left|\frac{dQ_F}{dQ_C}\right| = \frac{MPL_F}{MPL_C} = \frac{P_C}{P_F} \quad \text{(PPF slope = relative price)}$$
