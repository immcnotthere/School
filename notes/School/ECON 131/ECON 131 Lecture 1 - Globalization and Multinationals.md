> Why study international finance ?
>
> An event study

> **In 2022, the U.S. Dollar reached its strongest level in decades.**

**Figure (my description):** a line chart titled "Trade-Weighted U.S. Dollar Index (Broad)", index level from 110 to 130 over 2000 to 2023. The line is mostly 112 to 122, then climbs sharply to a peak near 130 in late 2022, marked "Late 2022 Peak: Strongest since 1985". A box titled "Bilateral Impact (1-Year Appreciation)" reads: vs. British Pound (£): +9%; vs. Euro (€): +7%; vs. Japanese Yen (¥): +11.5%.

**Claude's explanation (not in the handout):** a **trade-weighted** index averages the dollar's value against many currencies, weighted by how much the US trades with each ([[Trade-Weighted Dollar Index]]). Appreciation here means the dollar bought more pounds, euros and yen, so US exports became costlier abroad and imports cheaper (the same direction as the −20% / +25% example earlier).

**Claude's explanation (not in the handout):**
- **Trade openness** is trade divided by GDP: $\text{openness} = \dfrac{\text{exports} + \text{imports}}{\text{GDP}}$ (the slides do not give the formula; check the textbook). See [[Trade Openness]].
- **Financial (capital account) openness** measures how freely money can move across the border: [[Financial Openness (Capital Account Openness)]]. Industrial countries are near fully open; less developed countries and emerging markets are about halfway.
- The same theme runs through your Kimchi premium notes: openness is what lets arbitrage close gaps. A closed capital account (Korea's limits) leaves the gap open.

## Slide 15: multinational corporations
> MULTINATIONAL CORPORATIONS (MNC)
> • A MNC consists of a parent company in the firm's originating country and operating subsidiaries, branches and affiliates abroad
> • How they enter foreign markets
> – Exporting/Importing
> – Licensing: gives local firms the right to manufacture their products in exchange for fees
> – Franchising: provides sales or service strategies in exchange for fees
> – Joint venture: two or more firms form a new legal entity, jointly owned by all of the firms
> – FDI: sets up production facilities by establishing new operations or acquiring business abroad
> – Greenfield: starts a company from scratch

**Claude's explanation (not in the handout):** the list runs from least commitment (exporting) to most (FDI, greenfield). Concept notes: [[Multinational Corporation (MNC)]], [[MNC Foreign Market Entry Modes]].

> GLOBALIZATION: BENEFACTOR OR MENACE?

> TRADE GLOBALIZATION: BENEFACTOR OR MENACE?
