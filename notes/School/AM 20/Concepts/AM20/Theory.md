# Theory

A **theory** is a framework of assumptions and rules that describes how a system behaves. In science and mathematics, theory is always in conversation with data — theory says what *ought* to happen, data says what *did* happen.

> [!quote] The core tension
> Science is roughly the intersection of theory and data. Theory describes the general shape of behavior. Data pins it to reality.

## Theory vs. Fact

| | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Fact** | A discrete, observed measurement | Population was 50 in 2020 |
| **Theory** | A rule describing how facts relate | Population grows proportionally to size |

Facts are what you count. Theory is what you recognize when you step back.

## Theory in Mathematics

Mathematical theory operates by:

1. Stating **axioms** — things assumed true without proof
2. Deriving **theorems** — consequences that must follow logically
3. Building **models** — equations that translate theory into computable form

In differential equations, the theory is that *change is proportional to state* — and the models are the ODEs that follow from that assumption.

## Why Theory Needs Calculus

Theory describes how things change. Change requires derivatives. Derivatives require calculus. This is why differential equations appear the moment any scientific theory is made precise:

$$\text{behave} \implies \text{change} \implies \text{derivatives} \implies \text{ODEs}$$

> [!note] Connection to the course
> Every ODE in AM20 is a theory written down. Newton's second law, exponential growth, the law of cooling — each is a theoretical claim about how a variable changes, translated into an equation.
