## How the pieces assemble

Every exam skill gets built the same way. This is your own rule from 10-13: do the first problem of a kind slowly until you have the engine, skip same-format repeats, stop only for edge cases.

1. **Tool**: the one Review-sheet problem the next step needs (example: integration by parts right before HW2 Q4).
2. **Engine**: the lecture's worked example. Read it once, redo it closed-book, compare with the printed solution. What you get out: the method's steps in your own words.
3. **Graded HW**: same method, no printed solution. Check your answer by plugging it back in (skill D1), or with the textbook answer key (table below), after you finish.
4. **Edge**: one supplementary problem with a twist the HW does not have.
5. **Mock exam** (Sunday): unseen problems, 65 minutes, no notes. What you get out: a miss list.
6. **Fix** (Monday): redo only the missed steps.

The skills also feed each other, so the order is:
tools, then D2 (is it linear?), D4, D1 (check the D4 answers), D5, D9 (uses D4 or D5), D6 (uses D4's canonical form), D7 (needs partial derivatives), D8 (needs D7, and finding $\mu$ is a separable equation), then the mixed mock (that is D10), then fix.

## Monday trap list (20 min)
Each one is a mistake the handouts themselves warn about.

1. Divide by whatever multiplies $y'$ before reading off $p$ and $g$ (L3 sec 3.1). HW2 Q2, Q4 and HW3 Q1 need this.
2. $\mu = e^{\int p\,dt}$. It is not $\int p\,dt$, and it is not $e^{-\int p\,dt}$. That minus sign belongs to Abel's formula in L8, which is a different thing.
3. Once you have $(\mu y)' = \mu g$, integrate both sides. Differentiating again goes backwards (L3 Remark 2).
4. Dividing by an expression in $y$ can lose a constant solution. Check it (L1 sec 1.5 Step 3; HW2 Q6).
5. Pick the $\pm$ branch that passes through the initial point (L4 Ex2 Step 5).
6. Interval of validity: drop points where the square root goes negative or the original equation divides by zero, then keep the piece containing the initial point (L4 Ex2 Step 6).
7. Existence without solving: canonical form first, then find where $p$ or $g$ break ($\ln t$ needs $t > 0$; $\tan t$ breaks at $\frac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$), then keep the piece containing $t_0$ (L3 supp P10).
8. Exact: integrating $M$ in $x$ leaves an unknown $h(y)$, not a constant. The lecture calls writing $c$ there the most common error (L6 Ex3 Step 3).
9. $\mu(x)$ works only if $\frac{M_y - N_x}{N}$ has no $y$ in it. Otherwise try $\mu(y)$ with $\frac{N_x - M_y}{M}$ (L6 sec 6.3).
10. Models: rate in minus rate out, with units on both sides. The volume is constant only if the inflow rate equals the outflow rate (L5 Ex1 Step 1). Multiplying an inequality by a negative number flips it (L5 Ex1 (d)).
