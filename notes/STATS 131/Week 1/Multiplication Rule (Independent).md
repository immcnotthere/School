## Summary
If $A$ and $B$ are independent, knowing one happened does not change the probability of the other.

## Independence definition
$$
P(A\mid B)=P(A) \quad \text{and} \quad P(B\mid A)=P(B)
$$

## Multiplication formula (independent events)
$$
P(A\cap B)=P(A)\,P(B)
$$

## Quick test for independence
If $P(A)>0$ and $P(B)>0$, independence is equivalent to:
$$
P(A\cap B)=P(A)P(B)
$$

## Example
If $P(A)=0.20$ and $P(B)=0.30$ and independent:
$$
P(A\cap B)=0.06
$$

## Common mistakes
- Assuming independence because events are "unrelated" in plain language.
- Confusing independence with disjointness.
