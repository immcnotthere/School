# L1
1.1

**To rigorously model behavior in any domain, you must understand calculus.**

Here is the logical progression of that idea broken down:

1. **Theory Relies on Behavior:** Every field is built on theory and observation, and theories are formed by observing how things behave.
    
2. **Behavior is Change:** At its core, to observe behavior is simply to observe _change_.
    
3. **Change is a Derivative:** In mathematics, instantaneous change is captured by a derivative (e.g., $\frac{dy}{dx}$).
    
    - The **numerator ($dy$)** is your variable of focus (the thing that is changing).
        
    - The **denominator ($dx$)** is your normalizing variable (the context, or the "per unit" baseline you are measuring against, like time or distance).
        
4. **The Conclusion:** Because behavior is change, and the universal language of change is the derivative, a complete understanding of behavior requires the ability to write and interpret derivative equations.
    

In short: You cannot truly measure the "what" and "why" of a system without the mathematical tools to measure "how fast" and "relative to what."

Time is the most frequent variable of change so for any system where what happens tomorrow is meaningful derivatives will be meaningful.
