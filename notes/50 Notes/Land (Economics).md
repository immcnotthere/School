# Land (T)

---

## Definition

> **Land** encompasses all natural factor inputs used in production — agricultural land, natural resources, mineral deposits, and other gifts of nature. In trade theory it is denoted **T**.

Land is the quintessential [[Specific Factor]]: by its nature it cannot be physically relocated across sectors. A wheat field cannot become a clothing factory. In the [[Specific Factors Model]], Land is permanently tied to the **Food (F) sector**.

---

## Land in the [[Specific Factors Model]]

### Role

Land is the **specific factor** tied to the **Food (F) sector**:

$$Q_F = Q_F(T,\ L_F)$$

- T is **fixed** — it cannot move to the Clothing sector
- $L_F$ is variable — [[Labor]] flows freely between sectors

### Factor Immobility

| Factor | Mobility | Sector |
|--------|----------|--------|
| [[Capital]] (K) | ❌ Immobile (Specific) | Clothing |
| **Land (T)** | ❌ Immobile (Specific) | Food |
| [[Labor]] (L) | ✅ Mobile | Both |

---

## Marginal Product of Land

Under [[Diminishing Marginal Returns]], the marginal product of Land in the Food sector is:

$$MPT_F = \frac{\partial Q_F}{\partial T}$$

- $MPT_F \downarrow$ as T increases (holding $L_F$ fixed)
- $MPT_F \uparrow$ as $L_F$ increases — [[Factor Complementarity]]: more Labor makes each unit of Land more productive

---

## The Return to Land

In a [[Perfectly competitive markets|perfectly competitive]] market, Land earns its **marginal revenue product**:

$$\rho = P_F \cdot MPT_F$$

where $\rho$ is the **rental rate of Land** (sometimes called the land rent).

---

## Effect of Trade on the Return to Land

From [[Econ 140 – Lecture 9: The Specific Factors Model|Lecture 9]]:

Suppose the [[Small Open Economy]] has a [[Comparative Advantage]] in Clothing and opens to trade:

$$\left(\frac{P_C}{P_F}\right)^A < \left(\frac{P_C}{P_F}\right)^T$$

1. $P_F \downarrow$ (relatively) as the SOE imports food at lower world prices
2. [[Labor]] flows out of Food into Clothing: $L_F \downarrow$
3. [[Factor Complementarity]]: losing $L_F$ reduces $MPT_F$
4. Therefore:

$$\rho = P_F \cdot MPT_F \downarrow\downarrow$$

Both $P_F$ and $MPT_F$ fall → **Landowners are unambiguous losers** from trade.

> **General Principle:** Specific factors in the **import-competing sector lose** when a country opens to trade.

---

## Historical Example: The Corn Laws

This prediction explains a famous episode in trade policy history:

- **1830s UK**: Britain exported manufactures and imported food
- British [[Landowners]] were specific factors in the food (import-competing) sector
- Opening to food imports would lower $P_F$ → reduce land rents $\rho$
- Therefore **Landowners lobbied to maintain the [[Corn Laws]]** — tariffs on grain imports
- The Corn Laws were eventually repealed in **1846**, dealing a blow to landed interests

$$\text{Import food freely} \Rightarrow P_F \downarrow \Rightarrow \rho \downarrow \Rightarrow \text{Landowners lobby for tariffs}$$

---

## Real vs. Nominal Return

The real return to Land is unambiguously negative when the food sector contracts:

$$\text{Real return} = \frac{\rho}{P} = \frac{P_F \cdot MPT_F}{P}$$

Because $\rho$ falls while prices of both goods are relevant to consumption, landowners lose purchasing power regardless of their consumption bundle.

| Model | Treatment of Land |

| [[Hecksher-Ohlin Model|Heckscher-Ohlin Model]] | Sometimes included as a third factor alongside K and L |

## Key Equations

$$Q_F = Q_F(T, L_F)$$

$$MPT_F = \frac{\partial Q_F}{\partial T}$$

$$\rho = P_F \cdot MPT_F \quad \text{(rental rate / land rent)}$$

$$\rho \downarrow \text{ when } P_F \downarrow \text{ (import-competing sector shrinks)}$$
