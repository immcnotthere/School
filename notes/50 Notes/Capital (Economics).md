# Capital (K)

---

## Definition

> **Capital** refers to physical inputs used in production that are themselves produced goods — machines, equipment, factories, and infrastructure. In trade theory it is denoted **K**.

Capital is distinct from [[Labor]] (a human input) and [[Land]] (a natural input). Unlike [[Labor]], capital typically cannot immediately relocate across sectors or industries — making it a **[[Specific Factor]]** in short-to-medium run models.

---

## Capital in the [[Specific Factors Model]]

### Role

In the [[Specific Factors Model]], Capital is the **specific factor** tied to the **Clothing (C) sector**:

$$Q_C = Q_C(K,\ L_C)$$

- K is **fixed** — it cannot move to the Food sector
- $L_C$ is variable — [[Labor]] flows freely between sectors

### Why Specific?

Capital equipment built for one industry (e.g. textile looms, semiconductor fabs) has very **low value** if redeployed elsewhere. In the short run, it is effectively immobile.

| Factor | Mobility | Sector |
|--------|----------|--------|
| **Capital (K)** | ❌ Immobile (Specific) | Clothing |
| [[Land]] (T) | ❌ Immobile (Specific) | Food |
| [[Labor]] (L) | ✅ Mobile | Both |

---

## Marginal Product of Capital

Under [[Diminishing Marginal Returns]], the marginal product of Capital in the Clothing sector is:

$$MPK_C = \frac{\partial Q_C}{\partial K}$$

- $MPK_C \downarrow$ as K increases (holding $L_C$ fixed)
- $MPK_C \uparrow$ as $L_C$ increases — [[Factor Complementarity]]: more Labor makes each unit of Capital more productive

---

## The Return to Capital

In a [[Perfectly competitive markets|perfectly competitive]] market, Capital is paid its **marginal revenue product**:

$$r = P_C \cdot MPK_C$$

where $r$ is the **rental rate** of Capital.

---

## Effect of Trade on the Return to Capital

From [[Econ 140 – Lecture 9: The Specific Factors Model|Lecture 9]]:

Suppose the [[Small Open Economy]] has a [[Comparative Advantage]] in Clothing and opens to trade:

$$\left(\frac{P_C}{P_F}\right)^A < \left(\frac{P_C}{P_F}\right)^T$$

1. $P_C \uparrow$ as the SOE faces higher world prices for Clothing
2. More [[Labor]] flows into Clothing: $L_C \uparrow$
3. [[Factor Complementarity]]: additional $L_C$ raises $MPK_C$
4. Therefore:

$$r = P_C \cdot MPK_C \uparrow\uparrow$$

Both $P_C$ and $MPK_C$ rise → **Capital owners are unambiguous winners** from trade.

> **General Principle:** Specific factors in the **export sector gain** when a country opens to trade.

---

## Real vs. Nominal Return

The **real return** to Capital depends on what Capital owners consume:

$$\text{Real return} = \frac{r}{P} = \frac{P_C \cdot MPK_C}{P}$$

Since $r$ rises by more than either $P_C$ or $P_F$, Capital owners' purchasing power rises unambiguously — even accounting for higher import goods prices.

---

## Capital Across Models

| Model | Treatment of Capital |
|-------|---------------------|
| [[Ricardian Model]] | No capital — only [[Labor]] |
| [[Specific Factors Model]] | K is sector-specific (short run) |
| [[Hecksher-Ohlin Model|Heckscher-Ohlin Model]] | K is mobile across sectors (long run) |

The [[Specific Factors Model]] can be thought of as a **short-run** version of the [[Hecksher-Ohlin Model|Heckscher-Ohlin Model]], where Capital has not yet had time to reallocate.

---

## Key Equations

$$Q_C = Q_C(K, L_C)$$

$$MPK_C = \frac{\partial Q_C}{\partial K}$$

$$r = P_C \cdot MPK_C \quad \text{(rental rate of capital)}$$

$$r \uparrow \text{ when } P_C \uparrow \text{ (export sector expands)}$$
