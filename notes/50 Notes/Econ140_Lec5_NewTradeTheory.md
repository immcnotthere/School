## Two Types of Trade

### Inter-Industry Trade

> Trade *between* different industries (e.g., food for automobiles)

- Based on **[[Comparative Advantage]]**
- Arises when countries differ in **Opportunity Costs** due to:
  - Differences in relative productivities → **[[Ricardian Model]]**
  - Differences in relative resource endowments → **[[Hecksher-Ohlin Model|Heckscher-Ohlin Model]]**

**Example (H-O):** If clothing is capital-intensive and food is labor-intensive, a capital-abundant Home will export clothing; a labor-abundant Foreign will export food. This is a world *without* increasing returns — simple exchange of different goods.

---

### Intra-Industry Trade

> Trade *within* the same industry (e.g., Fords for Mercedes, Toyotas, & Volvos)

- Based on **[[Economies of Scale]]**
- Can arise between countries with **similar** economies — no comparative advantage needed
- **Importance:** Between 1/4 and 1/3 of all international trade is intra-industry trade

**U.S. Golf Club Example:**
- The U.S. *imports* golf clubs from China, Mexico, Vietnam, Japan, Taiwan, etc.
- The U.S. *also exports* golf clubs to Japan, Canada, Korea, Australia, Hong Kong, UK, etc.
- Both flows occur simultaneously — this is intra-industry trade in action.

## "New" Trade Theory

### Motivation

Standard comparative advantage theory (Ricardian / H-O) cannot easily explain:
1. The large volume of trade **among similar industrial countries**
2. The prevalence of **two-way (intra-industry) trade** in differentiated products

> *"...many people have concluded that a new framework for analyzing trade is needed."*  
> — [[Paul Krugman]] (1980), Nobel Prize 2008

### Three Pillars

1. **Product Differentiation** — firms produce different varieties; consumers value variety
2. **Imperfect Competition** — firms are price-setters (not price-takers); each faces a downward-sloping demand curve → [[Monopolistic Competition]]
3. **Economies of Scale** — average cost per unit falls as output rises; opening to trade expands the market and allows firms to exploit these economies

---

## Assumptions

- **Two identical countries:** Home and Foreign (*)
- **Market structure:** [[Monopolistic Competition]]
  - Many (N) small firms per country
  - Firms produce **differentiated products** (varieties)
  - **Free entry and exit**
- **Economies of scale:** TC = F + cQ (cost per unit falls with output)
- **Symmetric firms:** Each firm faces identical demand, technologies, and costs — differentiated only by product label

---

## Market Structure: Monopolistic Competition

| Feature | Perfect Competition | Monopolistic Competition |
|---|---|---|
| Price behavior | Price *taker* | Price *setter* |
| Products | Identical (perfect substitutes) | Differentiated |
| Demand curve | Perfectly elastic (horizontal) | Downward-sloping |
| Long-run profits | Zero | Zero (free entry) |
| P vs MC | P = MC | P > MC |

**Rearranged (inverse demand):**

$$P = P_{avg} + \frac{1}{Nb} - \frac{Q}{Sb}$$

**Symmetric equilibrium:** Since all firms are identical, $P = P_{avg}$, which implies every firm captures equal market share: $Q = S/N$.

**Slope of demand:** $-1/(Sb)$ — demand is flatter when products are more substitutable (high $b$).

### Marginal Revenue

$$MR(Q) = P(Q) + P'(Q) \cdot Q = P - \frac{Q}{Sb}$$

*(Recall: for linear demand $P = a - bQ$, $MR = a - 2bQ$)*

---

## Supply: Production and Cost

**Cost function for a typical firm:**

$$TC = F + cQ$$

- $F$ = fixed cost (overhead, R&D, setup)
- $c$ = constant marginal cost
- $AC = TC/Q = F/Q + c$ → average cost is *declining* in $Q$ (**economies of scale**)
- $MC = c$ (constant)

---

## Equilibrium

### PP Curve

*(Profit-maximizing Price as a function of N)*

Setting $MR = MC$, and using $Q = S/N$ in equilibrium:

$$MR = P - \frac{1}{Nb} = c$$

$$\boxed{P = c + \frac{1}{Nb}} \quad \text{(PP)}$$

- **Markup** = $P - c = \frac{1}{Nb}$
- Markup is **decreasing in $b$** (more substitutable → lower markup)
- Markup is **decreasing in $N$** (more firms → stronger competition → lower markup)
- PP curve is **upward-sloping** in (N, P) space

### CC Curve

*(Zero-profit / Average Cost condition)*

With free entry, profits = 0 in equilibrium, so $P = AC$:

$$AC = \frac{TC}{Q} = \frac{F + Qc}{Q} = \frac{F}{Q} + c = \frac{FN}{S} + c$$

$$\boxed{P = \frac{FN}{S} + c} \quad \text{(CC)}$$

- AC increases with $N$ (more firms → each produces less → higher unit cost)
- AC decreases with $S$ (larger market → more output per firm → lower unit cost)
- CC curve is **upward-sloping** in (N, P) space (but steeper than PP)

---

## Autarky Equilibrium

The equilibrium $(N^*, P^*)$ is found at the **intersection of PP and CC**:

$$P^* = c + \frac{1}{N^*b} = \frac{FN^*}{S} + c$$

Solving for $N^*$:

$$\frac{1}{N^*b} = \frac{FN^*}{S} \implies (N^*)^2 = \frac{S}{bF}$$

$$\boxed{N^* = \sqrt{\frac{S}{bF}}}$$

**Dynamics toward equilibrium:**
- If $N < N^*$: $P > AC$ → positive profits → firms **enter** → N rises, price falls, AC rises → converges to $N^*$
- If $N > N^*$: $P < AC$ → losses → firms **exit** → N falls → converges to $N^*$

**Long-run equilibrium properties:**
- Firms earn **zero profit**
- But $P > MC$ (monopolistic markup remains)

---

## Trade Equilibrium

When countries open to trade, the **relevant market size S increases** (firms can sell to both Home and Foreign consumers).

**Effects:**
1. **CC curve shifts down** (larger S → lower AC for any given N)
2. New equilibrium has **more firms** selling to the combined market: **variety-enhancing effect**
3. Equilibrium **prices fall**: **pro-competitive effect** (some domestic firms exit as they face negative profits from increased competition)
4. Each surviving firm **produces more** → lower average cost (**economies of scale**)
5. Total world firms > either country's autarky count, but < sum of both autarky counts

**Welfare:** Since consumers value variety and prices fall, **trade increases welfare in both countries**.

### Key Summary of Trade Effects

| Outcome | Direction |
|---|---|
| Market size S | ↑ |
| Number of varieties available to consumers | ↑ |
| Equilibrium price | ↓ |
| Number of domestic firms | ↓ (some exit) |
| Output per surviving firm | ↑ |
| Average cost per firm | ↓ |
| Consumer welfare | ↑ |

## Example: Automobile Industry

**Parameters:**
- $S = 900{,}000$ (Home), $S^* = 1{,}600{,}000$ (Foreign)
- $b = 1/30{,}000$
- $F = \$750{,}000{,}000$
- $c = \$5{,}000$

### Autarky

**Number of firms:**

$$N_{Home} = \sqrt{\frac{900{,}000}{750{,}000{,}000 \times \frac{1}{30{,}000}}} = \sqrt{\frac{900{,}000}{25{,}000}} = \sqrt{36} = 6$$

$$N_{Foreign}^* = \sqrt{\frac{1{,}600{,}000}{25{,}000}} = \sqrt{64} = 8$$

**Prices:**

$$P_{Home} = 5{,}000 + \frac{1}{6 \times \frac{1}{30{,}000}} = 5{,}000 + 5{,}000 = \$10{,}000$$

$$P_{Foreign}^* = 5{,}000 + \frac{1}{8 \times \frac{1}{30{,}000}} = 5{,}000 + 3{,}750 = \$8{,}750$$

**Autarky Results (Table 6-2):**

| | Home (Before Trade) | Foreign (Before Trade) |
|---|---|---|
| Total sales of autos | 900,000 | 1,600,000 |
| Number of firms | 6 | 8 |
| Sales per firm | 150,000 | 200,000 |
| Average cost | $10,000 | $8,750 |
| Price | $10,000 | $8,750 |

> Note: Foreign — with a larger market — has **lower prices**, **more variety**, and **higher output per firm** even before trade.

### Free Trade

World market size: $S^W = 900{,}000 + 1{,}600{,}000 = 2{,}500{,}000$

$$N^W = \sqrt{\frac{2{,}500{,}000}{25{,}000}} = \sqrt{100} = 10$$

$$P^W = 5{,}000 + \frac{1}{10 \times \frac{1}{30{,}000}} = 5{,}000 + 3{,}000 = \$8{,}000$$

**Post-Trade Results (Table 6-2):**

| | Home (Before Trade) | Foreign (Before Trade) | Integrated (After Trade) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total sales of autos | 900,000 | 1,600,000 | 2,500,000 |
| Number of firms | 6 | 8 | **10** |
| Sales per firm | 150,000 | 200,000 | 250,000 |
| Average cost | $10,000 | $8,750 | **$8,000** |
| Price | $10,000 | $8,750 | **$8,000** |

**Key takeaways:**
- World firm count (10) < sum of autarky firms (14) → consolidation/exit
- Both countries gain: **more variety at lower prices**
- Home benefits more (was the smaller, higher-price market)
- Some firms must **shut down** as a result of trade (creative destruction)
