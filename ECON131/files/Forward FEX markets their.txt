what is a forward exchange rate: From memory, it's a way to hedge future risk for fex-rate volatility for transactions that will occur. You pay a rate for certain loss for fear of greater uncertain loss, but risk uncertain gain. Delivery occurs on a 90-day forward contract on the 90th day where you have an obligation (not the option) to buy the currency at an agreed upon rate traditionally from a bank such that the bank should make a profit regardless relative to the price of the currency at the time of the contract's formation, It is on that day that you buy presumably more currency than your base currency would buy after 90-days successfully allowing you to have hedged the risk, however, it could be that your currency could be able to now buy the same or more not less, making the forward a loss for you. Alternatively, you could have guessed correctly, and the bank takes a loss having sold an asset (in currency) for less than it's worth (i.e. the dollar could buy 100 yen 90 days ago, but now it can buy 90 yen. The bank forwards at 95 even tho it is losing 5 yen relative to the spot rate price because of this trade.)
